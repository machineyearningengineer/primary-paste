## What this allows

- Use, inspect, modify and sell copies.
- Distribute covered software with its complete corresponding source and GPL notices.
- Keep private modifications private when they are not distributed.

Official artifacts include the license and identify their source commit. A source archive accompanies each binary release. An npm wrapper does not remove the app’s GPL obligations.

## Forks and branding

Forks should identify their own maintainer and builds. Use a distinct name, icon, bundle ID and signing identity; do not imply official endorsement. This branding guidance does not limit GPL rights. No registered trademark is claimed.

## Why this choice

The maintainer wants distributed improvements shared as source and accepts clearly identified commercial forks. GPL matches that intent. It does not prevent competition; AGPL’s network-use requirement adds little to this local utility.

Code the maintainer owns can be offered under other terms later. Existing GPL grants remain valid, and other copyright holders’ consent may be needed for their work.
